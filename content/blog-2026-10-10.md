# How to Optimize Your Website for Google AI Overviews and AI Mode

If you run a small website, you have probably seen Google answer a question before the blue links appear. Those answers live in generative AI features such as [AI Overviews and AI Mode](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). Google no longer treats this as a mystery. In May 2026 it published a guide on [optimizing for generative AI in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing). In June it added [Search Console reports](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports). By 31 August 2026 those reports and a site-wide inclusion control were [available worldwide](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024).

You do not need a new acronym or a secret markup file. You need useful pages, a crawlable site, and a way to see whether those pages appear in AI answers.

## Why this matters for small businesses right now

People still search Google for a plumber, a course, a product, or a how-to. Many of those searches now show a generated summary with links. Google says those summaries are grounded in the same index and ranking systems as ordinary results. If your page cannot be crawled, indexed, and shown with a snippet, it cannot be cited in the AI answer.

That favors sites that already do the basics. It hurts stacks of thin “ultimate guides.” Google’s 2026 guide puts unique, [non-commodity content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) first. A bakery should explain why its proofing schedule changed this summer, not republish “seven bread tips.” A trainer should recap mistakes from the last cohort, not paste a generic syllabus.

## SEO is still the job. AEO and GEO are extra labels.

Vendors now sell answer engine optimization (AEO) and generative engine optimization (GEO). Google’s position is blunt: [optimizing for generative AI search is still SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). AI Overviews and AI Mode use retrieval-augmented generation: the model looks up pages in Search, then writes from those pages. Query fan-out can expand one question into related lookups. Google’s lawn-weeds example also retrieves herbicide, chemical-free, and prevention pages.

Do not publish a new article for every related wording. Creating pages mainly to manipulate rankings or AI answers can violate [scaled content abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) policy. Write for the job the reader needs done. If an “AEO package” is mostly files, chunks, and mention campaigns, compare it with Google’s mythbusting section before you pay.

## First, confirm your pages are even eligible

Visibility in AI features has two gates that many small sites skip.

### Indexed pages that can show a snippet

Google states that a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet. That means:

- Google can crawl the URL.
- The page is not blocked by `noindex`.
- Snippets are allowed. Do not use `nosnippet` if you want the page cited.
- The site meets the [Search technical requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/technical).

Indexing is not guaranteed even when you follow the rules. Still, most eligibility failures on small sites are simpler: the important page lives behind a login, is orphaned with no internal links, returns an error, or is a duplicate of another URL.

### The Search Console generative AI control

A second rule is newer. Your property must be included in Search generative AI features. Google rolled this [control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024) out worldwide on 31 August 2026. The default is include, so most verified sites are already eligible.

Open Search Console, go to **Settings**, then **Search generative AI**. Keep **Include** unless you have a legal or brand reason to stay out. Exclude hides your links from AI Overviews, AI Mode, and generative AI in Discover. Google says this is not a ranking signal for the rest of Search. It does not replace [Google-Extended](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/overview-google-crawlers) for training limits, or `noindex` if you want to leave Search. Child properties can inherit an exclude setting from a parent domain, so check inheritance on subfolders and subdomains.

## Write pages only you could write

Google’s AI-search advice is the same quality bar as before, with sharper language about commodity pages. Commodity content restates common knowledge. Non-commodity content adds experience: a measurement, a failure, a constraint, a photo of the job, or a decision a reader cannot get from a web-wide summary.

Test the next draft: could a generic model write this without visiting your business? Does a heading match a question a customer asked this month? Would a first-time visitor feel finished? If the first answer is yes, add the missing details. Google contrasts generic “tips for first-time homebuyers” with a first-hand story about waiving an inspection and checking a sewer line. A web studio can publish the contrast failures it found on a real site. A marketer can show which form fields were removed and why. A coding instructor can walk through the exact error that stopped a student’s project.

Images and video can appear in AI features too. Follow [image SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) and [video SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video) instead of stuffing stock photos.

AI drafting is fine only when the published page meets [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials) and spam policies. Google’s [guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) notes that models predict likely words; they do not retrieve facts. Fact-check titles, meta descriptions, alt text, and structured data. Mass-producing pages without added value is still scaled content abuse.

## Keep the technical foundation boring and solid

AI features read the same crawlable web Google already uses. Markup will not rescue a blocked or duplicated site.

Verify the site in [Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console), submit an accurate sitemap, and fix coverage errors on money pages first. Put important copy in HTML Google can see after JavaScript runs. If a service description appears only after a click crawlers never fire, AI Search never sees it. Follow [JavaScript SEO](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics) if you use a modern framework. Use headings for people; Google says you do not need perfect semantic code. Cut duplicate URLs. Make the page usable on a phone, with the main answer above the newsletter wall.

Write one clear page for humans. You do not need a separate “AI version.”

A practical rewrite looks like this. A software training page that says “We offer Python courses for beginners and professionals” is commodity copy. A stronger page names the audience (“working analysts who have never written a function”), the format (six evening sessions, homework in a shared repo), a constraint (classes stay under twelve people), proof (the error logs students hit in week two), and a next step (a sample lesson and enrollment dates). That page can satisfy a human and still be the kind of source an AI overview can cite, because the details are not already on every competitor homepage.

## Local and shop details still feed AI answers

If you sell locally or online, generative answers can include business and product details. Google points owners to [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) and [Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants) feeds. Keep name, address, hours, categories, products, and photos accurate.

Do not invent schema “for AI.” Structured data is not required for generative AI Search. Keep using documented types when they qualify you for rich results.

## Myths you can skip for Google Search

Google’s May 2026 guide lists tactics that neither help nor hurt Search, or that waste time:

- **llms.txt and similar AI files.** Google Search does not use them. Other tools might. Creating one will not rank you in AI Overviews.
- **Chunking articles into tiny fragments.** Google can understand several topics on one page. Page length should match the reader, not a token window.
- **Rewriting copy in a special “AI voice.”** You do not need every long-tail synonym on the page.
- **Buying inauthentic mentions.** Spam systems still apply. Fake forum praise is not a shortcut into citations.
- **Over-investing in structured data as an AI hack.** Useful for rich results. Not a secret AI key.

Spend that time on a page that answers the next customer email in public.

## How to measure AI visibility in Search Console

Since 3 June 2026, Search Console has a [Generative AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports). Google says it reached all sites worldwide on 31 August 2026. Open **Performance**, then **Generative AI features**.

What you can see today:

- Impressions when links to your site appeared in AI Overviews or AI Mode.
- Pages, countries, devices, and dates.
- A web text search type and a multimodal type (Lens, Circle to Search, image uploads).
- A separate Discover generative AI report.

The June announcement and [help document](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) describe impressions. They do not present this report as a full replacement for clicks, queries, and conversions. Google has said it may add metrics later. If the report is missing, the usual causes are too few AI impressions or an exclude setting.

Treat impressions as a visibility signal. If AI features cite a thin blog post but never your service page, that service page may be weaker, blocked, or too vague. Google also states that no third-party tool has access to internal ranking or AI systems.

## A 30-day plan you can run without a large team

**Days 1–3: eligibility.** Verify Search Console, confirm Include is on, inspect key URLs, and fix `noindex`, robots blocks, and 404s.

**Days 4–10: one strong page per offer.** For your top services, write who it is for, what you do, a real constraint (price, timeline, tools, or location), proof, and a next step. Do not spin keyword clones.

**Days 11–18: usability.** Compress images, write alt text, put the answer near the top, and link from the homepage.

**Days 19–24: listings.** Update Business Profile hours and photos. Fix Merchant Center errors if you sell products.

**Days 25–30: measure.** Compare the generative AI report with ordinary Search performance. Improve cited pages with better evidence. Merge near-duplicates.

Repeat quarterly. The checklist does not depend on a ranking leak.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate SEO strategy for AI Overviews?

No. Google’s published guidance is that success in AI Overviews and AI Mode rests on the same ranking and quality systems as Search. Improve content quality, crawlability, and page experience. Then measure AI impressions in Search Console.

### Will appearing in an AI Overview send me fewer clicks?

This article cannot honestly predict click-through for your niche. Google’s materials emphasize that AI features still show prominent links to supporting pages, and that impressions from those features are now reportable. Watch your own Search Console data instead of assuming a universal traffic drop or lift.

### Does llms.txt help me appear in Google AI Mode?

Google says Search, including generative AI features, does not use llms.txt. Other products may read such files. For Google Search it is optional housekeeping, not a ranking tactic.

### Can I use ChatGPT or another model to write my blog?

You can use AI as a drafting aid if you add original value, check facts, and avoid publishing many low-effort pages. Google judges the result, not the tool. Unreviewed AI copy is a quality and spam risk.

### Why is my Generative AI performance report empty?

Common causes are too few impressions in AI features, or an exclude setting on the property or a parent property. The report also omits Search Labs experiments.

### Should I opt out of Search generative AI features?

Only if you have a clear reason to keep your content out of AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Discover’s generative features. Opting out does not hide you from the rest of Search, and other sites can still be cited for similar topics.

## Conclusion

Google’s 2026 documentation closed a lot of speculation. AI Overviews and AI Mode are Search features built on the index you already try to rank in. Unique pages, crawlable HTML, accurate business data, and Search Console measurement beat AEO folklore.

If you do one thing this week, open the generative AI control, then rewrite your weakest money page so it could only have come from your business.

## What to do next

Apply the 30-day checklist to one service page and one article, then recheck Search Console. When related articles are published here, use them to go deeper on technical SEO and people-first writing. Start the eligibility and rewrite steps on your live site this week.
